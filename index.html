The problem

Everyone’s been told to use AI. Almost nobody’s been taught.

People feel behind

Tech professionals we’ve talked to rate their own AI skill 1.5 out of 5.

Tutorials don’t stick

Watching a video about agents isn’t running one.

Real agents are risky

Agents that edit files can break them. Beginners need somewhere safe to practice.

Review falls behind

Agents ship faster than people can check.

2 in 3got no AI training at work

55% of US workers use AI regularly. Only 1 in 3 got any AI training from their employer in the last six months.

2 in 3 and 55%: The Conference Board, survey of about 1,300 US workers, 2026. 1.5 out of 5: informal self-ratings from about 400 conversations, not a formal survey.

Why now

Agents multiply output. They multiply the gaps too.

Three lessons from leading an engineering team through the shift to AI agents.

1

Leading beats prompting

Output jumped when people learned to plan, split and hand off work, not when they found a better prompt.

2

Review becomes the bottleneck

Unless review is built into the workflow, it quietly falls away.

3

One power user isn’t a team

Gains stick only when everyone has a path from first prompt to running a crew.
