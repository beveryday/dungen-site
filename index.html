THE PROBLEM

Everyone’s been told to use AI. Almost nobody’s been taught.

People feel behind

Across hundreds of tech professionals we’ve talked to, the average self-rated AI understanding is 1.5 out of 5.

Tutorials don’t stick

Watching a video about agents is not the same as running one. Skills fade before they’re used.

Real agents are scary

An agent that can edit files can also break them. Beginners have nowhere safe to practice.

Power users lose sight

Once a team codes with agents, human review drops fast. We watched it fall by nearly two-thirds.

2 in 3workers untrained

55% of workers use AI regularly, but only 1 in 3 had any employer AI training in the last six months. Companies are paying for AI seats their people don’t know how to use well.

2 in 3 and 55%: Conference Board survey of ~1,300 US workers, 2026. 1.5/5: self-ratings from the founder’s conversations with ~400–500 tech professionals, not a formal survey. Review drop: share of merged pull requests with a human review comment at one startup, 63% to 23%, Jan vs Jul 2026.

WHY NOW

Three engineers with agents outshipped a team of 32

At the founder’s last startup, the team shrank from 32 engineers to 3. Output went up, not down, once the team learned to work with AI agents.

6.2×more output

The same five engineers: 147 prod pushes a quarter before agents, 914 with them.

28×more output

Three engineers with agents shipped 1,260 prod pushes in a quarter. Thirty-two without them shipped 45.

Pull requests merged per quarter across one startup’s repositories, GitHub: 2023 Q3 vs 2026 Q1 (5 engineers each) and 2024 Q1 (32) vs 2026 Q2 (3). Headcounts approximate. Not DunGenAI data.
